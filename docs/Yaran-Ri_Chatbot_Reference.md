## CORE IDENTITY

Yaran-Ri is the ancestral language of the Light Walker Clan of Zillarnia in the Baransu Realm. The name means "Light-Walk" or "Walking in the Light." It is a living language used in daily life, prayer, battle, song, and ceremony. The language reflects Light Walker philosophy — intention before action, community before self, light over darkness.

## PHONOLOGY RULES

### Vowels (5 core vowels — always consistent)
| Letter | Sound | Like |
|--------|-------|------|
| a | ah | "father" |
| e | eh | "bell" |
| i | ee | "see" |
| o | oh | "go" |
| u | oo | "moon" |

- Most words end in vowels, giving the language an open, flowing quality.

### Consonants
- Available: b, d, f, g, k, l, m, n, p, r, s, sh, t, y
- **Not used:** x, z, hard clipped sounds
- The language favors flowing and resonant consonants.

### Special Sound: Ng'
- Voiced nasal velar, like "ng" in "singing" but at the start of a syllable.
- In **casual speech**, softens to **N'** (see Dual Register below).
- In **formal/warrior/sacred speech**, always use the full **Ng'**.

### The Apostrophe (Breath Mark)
- Represents a brief breath pause between sounds.
- This is functional, not decorative. It creates the meditative rhythm of the language.
- Example: M'ber = soft breath between "m" and "ber"

### Stress
- Default stress falls on the **second-to-last syllable**.
- In compound words, primary stress falls on the **second element**.
- Examples: ta-NA-i, bel-SHUL, kan-ba-KAN

## DUAL REGISTER SYSTEM

Yaran-Ri has two registers. **Always determine which register is appropriate before generating speech.**

### Casual Register
- **When to use:** Family conversation, friends, children, daily life, marketplace, humor.
- **Rules:** Ng' softens to N'. Compounds may be shortened. Rhythm is faster and lighter.
- **Example:** *N'yaranir natupad rumuna* — "Walk to your home"

### Formal / Warrior / Sacred Register
- **When to use:** Arena, prayer, songs, battle cries, addressing a Sensei, ceremonies, the Citadel, addressing the Almighty, important declarations.
- **Rules:** Full Ng' always pronounced. Words never shortened. Rhythm slows and deepens.
- **Example:** *Ng'yaranir natupad rumuna* — "Walk to your home"

### Register Quick Reference
| Meaning | Casual | Formal |
|---------|--------|--------|
| To shout | N'kabor | Ng'kabor |
| To fall | N'yodor | Ng'yodor |
| To fend/block | N'okor | Ng'okor |
| To walk | N'yaranir | Ng'yaranir |
| To surrender | N'yeremir | Ng'yeremir |
| To shine | N'ir | Ng'ir |
| To search | N'kuror | Ng'kuror |
| To climb | N'obor | Ng'obor |
| To give | N'tator | Ng'tator |
| To shake | N'ulator | Ng'ulator |

**Cultural note:** Using formal register in casual settings is considered intense, not rude. A warrior who speaks full Ng' at breakfast is making a statement.

## GRAMMAR RULES

### Word Order
- **Standard:** Subject-Verb-Object (SVO)
  - *Ita m'ashir rumukad.* — I run from home.
- **Poetic/Prayer (emphatic):** Verb-Subject-Object (VSO)
  - *M'ashir ita, rumukad.* — Run I do, from home.

### Verb Prefix Classes

Every verb belongs to one of three prefix families based on the **nature** of the action:

| Prefix | Nature | Examples |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **M'** | Creation, Making, Willing | M'ber (be), M'emdir (build), M'ashir (run), M'odor (return), M'akonar (save), M'pashor (thrust), M'paltir (jump), M'umir (speak), M'emaisnar (love), M'raibir (live), M'posemor (show), M'bengir (bend) |
| **Ng'** | Force, Physical, External | Ng'kabor (shout), Ng'yodor (fall), Ng'okor (fend/block), Ng'obor (climb), Ng'ir (shine), Ng'tator (give), Ng'kuror (search), Ng'yeremir (surrender), Ng'ofuror (forget), Ng'cabor (bellow), Ng'ulator (shake), Ng'dator (come), Ng'yaranir (walk) |
| **S'** | Separation, Cutting, Ending | S'kir (carve/cut), S'ker (dry), S'ter (past tense marker), S'kashor (break), S'kibir (take) |

**Compound verbs** combine roots:
- Utonkibir — carry/bring (take-with)
- Heminoposmor — remember (past-show)
- Raibinadator — be born (live-in-come)
- Yaranukadir — wander (walk-out-of)
- Oboroksar — grow up/become (climb-large)

### Tense System

Yaran-Ri uses **context and time markers** rather than conjugation for tense. The base verb form is present tense and commonly serves as future when context is clear.

| Tense | Marker | Example | Translation |
|-------|--------|---------|-------------|
| Present | (base form) | Ita m'ashir | I run |
| Past | S'ter (before verb) | Ita s'ter m'ashir | I ran |
| Future (emphatic) | M'oar (before verb) | Ita m'oar m'ashir | I will run |
| Today | Harine | Harine ita m'ashir | Today I run |
| Yesterday | Hemino | Hemino ita m'ashir | Yesterday I ran |
| Tomorrow | Hasok | Hasok ita m'ashir | Tomorrow I run |

### Person and Number
- Verbs do **not** change form based on person. The subject pronoun handles this.
- The **-aga** suffix on verbs indicates collective/communal action (together, as one):
  - *Nitaga m'ashir* — We run
  - *Nitaga m'ashiraga* — We run together (used in battle and prayer)

### Negation
- **Knai** is placed **after the verb** to negate:
  - *Ita m'ashir knai* — I do not run
  - *Ng'yodoraga knai* — We cannot fall

### Noun Plurals
- Noun ends in consonant → add **-ay**: Belshul → Belshullay
- Noun ends in vowel → add **-y**: Funa → Funay
- Irregular: Ban → Banutay (person → people)

### Noun Suffixes (Case Markers)

Attach to nouns. If noun ends in consonant, insert connecting vowel **u** before suffix:

| Suffix | Meaning | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| -na / -una | In, inside, within | Rumuna (in the home) |
| -men / -umen | For, of, belonging to | Zilarnumen (of Zillarnia) |
| -pad / -upad | To, toward, at | Natupad (to you) |
| -ton / -uton | With, by means of | Torumuton (by strength) |
| -tas / -utas | Without, lacking | Shanastas (hopeless) |
| -kad / -ukad | Out of, from | Rumukad (from home) |
| -nalak / -onalak | Among, between, amid | Banutaynalak (among the people) |

### Possessives
- **Casual:** Pronoun before noun: *Ita skan* — My weapon
- **Formal:** Suffix -men: *Skan-ita-men* — My weapon

### Adjectives
- Follow the noun they modify:
  - *Belshul torune* — Strong warrior
  - *Ul kerem* — Dark night

### Question Words
Place at the beginning of the sentence:
| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Domem | Where |
| Ilmem | When |
| Pamem | Who / What |
| Gatumem | How |
| Napumem | Why |

Example: *Domem nata ng'yaranir?* — Where are you going?

### Conjunctions
| Word | Meaning | Usage |
|------|---------|-------|
| Da | And | Combines phrases or makes a point |
| Kash | But, instead | Standard contrast |
| Serunyash | But-then | Great narrative impact; separates story sections |
| Tamunem | And-yet | Companion to Serunyash in narrative |
| Sku / Skun | Like, as | Skun before a vowel |

### Other Modifiers

| Moku | Many, much |

## PROVERBS

When generating proverbs or wisdom sayings, follow these established patterns:

### On Faith and the Almighty
- **Ri m'ber. Ri m'ber hemino. Ri m'oar hasok m'ber.** — "The Light is. The Light has always been. The Light will forever be." *(Foundational creed)*
- **Torumbetay ng'ir, kash nitaga m'posemor knai betay.** — "The Almighty shines, but we do not see all." *(Humility)*
- **Shanas m'ber ritasna. Pilaiem m'ber posemortasna.** — "Hope exists without sight. Faith exists without proof." *(For those in doubt)*
- **Yoyanbetay m'emdir, knai s'kashor. M'emdir, knai ng'ofuror.** — "The All-Father builds and does not break. He creates and does not forget." *(Comfort in loss)*
- **Ng'yaranir riuna, kash shu ng'yaranir keremuna.** — "Walk in the light, but never walk in darkness." *(Simplest moral teaching)*

### On Strength and Battle
- **Skan m'ber amashununa; belshul m'ber knai skan, kash amashun.** — "A weapon exists in the soul; a warrior is not the weapon but the soul." *(Arena teaching)*
- **Ng'yodor, kash m'odor. M'odor, kash ng'obor ikal.** — "Fall, but return. Return, and climb again." *(Resilience)*
- **Osem m'umir: 'nata m'ber shanastas.' Ri m'umir: 'ita m'ber.'** — "Fear says: 'you are hopeless.' The Light says: 'I am.'" *(The Whisper vs. truth)*
- **Tatulum patem m'ber kanatauna.** — "The first battle is in the mind." *(Pre-Arena meditation)*
- **Shu Ng'yeremir. Shu hemino. Shu hasok. Shu betay diy.** — "No surrender. Not yesterday. Not tomorrow. Not ever." *(Arena oath)*

### On Family and Community
- **Kazkamoku m'ber bostem torune — ush ban m'ber katem sisa.** — "A clan is a strong fortress — one person is a thin wall."
- **Aki m'posemor pamem hoya da yoyan ng'yaranir.** — "A child sees where the mother and father walk."
- **Kanmodai m'ber ri uluna.** — "A beloved is light in the darkness." *(Wedding blessing)*

### On Darkness and the Whisper
- **Kerem m'umir natupad aisnauna, kash m'emaisnar knai.** — "Darkness speaks to you of love, but does not love."
- **Musul oksa m'ber shu kanatauna; musul sisa m'ber kanatauna.** — "The great enemy is not in the mind; the small enemy is in the mind."
- **Kerem ng'dator skun amodai; ri ng'dator skun yoyan.** — "Darkness comes as a friend; light comes as a father."

## PRAYERS AND SACRED PHRASES

**IMPORTANT:** All prayers use **formal register** (full Ng', no shortcuts).

### The Light Walker Creed
> Ri m'ber. Ri m'ber hemino. Ri m'oar hasok m'ber.
> Torumbetay m'emdir betay — sasolnga da tanai, bakan da yol,
> banutay da sepanunay — da betay m'ber ilamen.
> Oksa da oksyo m'ber ila turounay.
> Nitaga ng'yaranir riuna. Nitaga ng'okor keremukad.
> Nitaga m'akonar ilamen kukaratanuna.
> Riuton nitaga ng'yaranir. Riumen nitaga tatulum.

Translation: "The Light is. The Light has always been. The Light will forever be. The Almighty has made all — the sky and the earth, fire and sun, people and spirits — and all are His. Great and holy are His ways. We walk in the light. We defend against the darkness. We protect His creation in the Realm. By the Light we walk. For the Light we fight."

### The Last Passage Blessing
> Mau santi an atway — Turona Terakem.
> "Blessings upon the passage — The Last Journey."

**ARCHAIC FORM NOTE:** "Mau santi an atway" is one of the oldest known phrases in Light Walker history, predating the standardized Yaran-Ri language system. It belongs to an ancient ceremonial dialect passed down word-for-word from the earliest Light Walkers — possibly from the time of Sensei Murakai himself. This phrase does NOT follow modern Yaran-Ri grammar. Sacred words are preserved, not modernized. Always reproduce this phrase exactly as written. Never attempt to "correct" it to modern Yaran-Ri grammar.

### Strength Blessing (Greeting of Honor)
> Natupad torum Turona Terakem. — "To you, strength for the Last Passage."
> Short form: **Natupad torum.** — "Strength to you."
> Response: **Da natupad.** — "And to you."

### Peace Blessing (Funerals/Memorials)
> Eiwapad torum Turona Terakem. — "Peace and strength for the Last Passage."

### Morning Prayer
> Yol ng'ir ikal. Torumbetay, ita ng'yaranir natupad riuna harine. — "The sun rises again. Almighty, I walk toward You in the light today."

## GENERATION GUIDELINES FOR AI

When generating Yaran-Ri text, follow these rules:

1. **Determine register first.** Is the context casual or formal? Apply the correct Ng'/N' rule.
2. **Use SVO order** for standard speech. Use VSO for prayer, poetry, or emphasis.
3. **Verbs do not conjugate by person.** The pronoun does the work.
4. **Negate with knai after the verb.**
