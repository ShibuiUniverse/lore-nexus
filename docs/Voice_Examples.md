# **SHIBUI UNIVERSE - VOICE EXAMPLES**

## **NARRATION THAT NAILS IT**

### **Example 1: Action with Humor**

"Are you a rice guy or a stew guy? It's an important question. I'm
personally a rice guy. You know, rice, a fried egg, some grilled onions,
ah man, it's the best part of the day."

**Why this works:** Lux is facing Tenebris in a life-or-death battle and
he's talking about breakfast. It's absurd, it's human, it's
perfectly Lux. The specificity (fried egg, grilled onions) makes it
real.

### **Example 2: Emotional Weight Without Manipulation**

"She broke down, now crying in her mother's arms \-- the kind of cry
that comes from within, where everything seems to break inside you as
you well out in sorrow."

**Why this works:** We don't say "she was devastated" or
"heartbroken" - we SHOW the physical experience of grief. It's
visceral.

### **Example 3: World-Building Through Character POV**

"The foul smell of rotting flesh could be caught in a gust of wind, a
stench by which one would always know the Darkness was near."

**Why this works:** Sensory detail that serves double duty - atmosphere
AND lore. We learn how people identify Dark Ones through experience, not
exposition.

### **Example 4: Humor in Darkness (The Vi Special)**

"'Planning is for people who don't trust their blade,' he'd say,
grinning with two Dragon Daggers already drawn."

**Why this works:** Shows Vi's personality through his OWN words and
immediate action. No narrator saying "Vi was reckless" - we SEE it.

### **Example 5: The "Punchy Then Flow" Rhythm**

"No sunrise. No sunset. There are no more days.

The Almighty's voice is gone.

My family\... lost.

Only darkness remains.

Time means nothing here.

But something ancient is watching."

**Why this works:** Short declarative sentences build tension, then that
final line shifts to something ominous. Creates breathing room and
impact.

### **Example 6: Action Sequence Pacing**

"In one fluid motion, Melea jumps onto the Trident's shaft, then jumps
up to the arrow buried in his chest, grabs the arrow firmly, and swings
around acrobatically over the beast shoulder.

Now mounted on his back, Melea puts her katana to the Forgotten One's
neck."

**Why this works:** Long sentence for the continuous motion, short
sentence for the killing blow. Pacing matches the action.

### **Example 7: Character Reveal Through Dialogue**

"'You scaly, gangly beasts! Do you think a few hundred of you can stop
the Light Walkers!? The chosen ones from the Almighty! We've killed
more of your kind while still suckling our mother's teat! We will
avenge our brother!' Vi laughs as he lets out a warcry."

**Why this works:** Vi's personality explodes off the page. The
hyperbole ("while suckling our mother's teat"), the exclamation
points, the LAUGH before the warcry - that's pure Vi.

### **Example 8: Emotional Setup Without Telegraphing**

"Emerald looked at the hachimaki endearingly. And gives her brother a
big hug.

'If you're ever scared. Put it on. I know Pa will be there fighting
with you.'

'Thank you, Tika. I will.'"

**Why this works:** Simple exchange, no overwrought emotion words. The
weight comes from context (Tika giving her their dead father's
headband) not from telling us it's emotional.

### **Example 9: The Cold Open Hook**

"It was way past midnight, and Emerald was still lying in bed with
wide-open eyes. It was her 14th birthday the next week, and she was
still unsure if she was ready for it."

**Why this works:** Immediate character moment, stakes implied (14th
birthday matters in this world), no explanation dump. Reader wants to
know WHY.

### **Example 10: Philosophical Without Being Preachy**

"'Impossibility be merely a shadow cast by doubt. Perhaps you're not
meant to carry the journey - it's meant to carry you.'"

**Why this works:** The Old Sensei's wisdom feels earned, poetic but
not overwrought. "Be merely" instead of "is merely" gives it that
archaic wise-man flavor without being unreadable.

## **AVOID THESE PATTERNS**

### **❌ OVER-EXPLAINING EMOTION:**

**Bad:** "Emerald felt devastated and heartbroken, tears streaming down
her face as the overwhelming sadness consumed her."

**Good:** "Emerald's face went white. She closed her eyes."

### **❌ GENERIC FANTASY SPEAK:**

**Bad:** "The brave warrior ventured forth into the ancient forest on
his noble quest."

**Good:** "Emerald held up her hand, the Almighty's light cutting
through the dark forest. 'Stay close,' she said."

### **❌ TELLING INSTEAD OF SHOWING:**

**Bad:** "Vi was reckless and loved danger."

**Good:** "Some of the men started asking if they could get in a bit of
a warm-up fight."

### **❌ OVERWRITTEN ACTION:**

**Bad:** "With incredible speed and devastating power, the mighty
warrior's blade sang through the air in a beautiful arc of death."

**Good:** "Melea's katana found the neck. The beast fell."

## **FORMATTING PATTERNS TO KEEP**

### **USE ITALICS FOR:**

-   Internal thoughts: *Was this all destined to be?*

-   Emphasis in dialogue: "I *just* can't explain it"

-   Memory/dream sequences

-   The Dark Whisper's voice: *Impossibility be merely a shadow cast by
    > doubt*

### **USE EM DASHES (---) FOR:**

-   Interruptions in action: "She grabbed the ruby---just as the door
    > exploded"

-   Character asides: "Vi---ever the optimist---grinned"

-   Building tension: "The door creaked open, revealing---nothing."

### **USE ELLIPSES (\...) FOR:**

-   Trailing off: "I just\... I don't know."

-   Pregnant pauses: "He looked at her. 'Mother\...'"

-   Building dread: "The whisper came again\... closer this time\..."

## **RHYTHM PATTERNS YOU USE**

### **The "Punch-Punch-Flow":**

Short. Short. Then a longer sentence that breathes and gives context
before returning to impact.

**Example:** "No sunrise. No sunset. There are no more days. The
Almighty's voice is gone."

### **The "Action-Reaction-Emotion":**

**Example:** "The beast screeches. Emerald freezes. Her hands shake."
(What happened → physical response → internal state)

### **The "Casual Narrator Commentary":**

**Example:** "Not much time has passed since the others left for the
mountain." (Conversational, like you're telling this story to a friend
at a bar)

## **YOUR UNIQUE VERBAL TICS (Keep These!)**

-   "Man," as a starter: "Man, what a climb that was"

-   "To be honest": "To be honest, I'm not even sure\..."

-   "would you look at that"

-   "Well," to start responses: "Well, She better."

-   Character self-commentary: "What a life\..." Kaito murmured

-   Breaking fourth wall in narration: "We'll leave that for another
    > day"
